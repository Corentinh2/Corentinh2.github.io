2023 · The centenary

The race of the century

The first 24 Hours of Le Mans took place in 1923. To celebrate 100 years of its race, the ACO (Automobile Club de l'Ouest) organised an exceptional edition in 2023.

For the occasion, major manufacturers returned to the top class with ever faster and more aerodynamic cars: Ferrari, Porsche, Cadillac and Peugeot joined Toyota and Glickenhaus.

The race was sold out, with more than 300,000 spectators. At the finish, the Ferrari 499P #51 won, 58 years after the Italian brand's previous victory.

Since 2023

What has changed

New manufacturers, departures, a new GT class and records: three eventful years in a nutshell.

🏆

Ferrari's hat-trick

The 499P won every race from 2023 to 2025. In 2025 the winner was even a privately entered car, AF Corse's #83.

🔁

Toyota is back

In 2026 the new Toyota TR010 #7 ended the Ferrari streak, giving the Japanese manufacturer its 6th Le Mans win.

🚀

Newcomers

BMW, Alpine, Lamborghini and Isotta Fraschini arrived in 2024, Aston Martin and its V12 Valkyrie in 2025, then Genesis in 2026.

👋

Departures

Glickenhaus left after 2023 and Lamborghini after 2024. At the end of 2025 Porsche withdrew its factory team from the World Championship.

🏁

Enter LMGT3

Since 2024 the GT class uses GT3 cars derived from road models. They race alongside the Hypercars and LMP2s.

👥

Record crowd

In 2024, 329,000 spectators attended the race, a record for the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

2026 grid

The Hypercar teams

Two rule sets coexist. LMH (Le Mans Hypercar) cars are designed freely by the manufacturer. LMDh cars share a standard chassis and hybrid system. A "Balance of Performance" adjusts weight and power so they all stay close, at around 680 hp.
