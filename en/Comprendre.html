The guide

Understandthe race

Three classes on track at the same time, strict driving rules and flags to know: here is the essential to follow the 24 Hours.

On track

Three classes, one race

All cars race together, but each class has its own ranking. Race numbers have a different colour for each class: the plate on each card shows an example.

Hypercar

The top class

Prototypes from the big manufacturers, mostly hybrid, at around 680 hp. They fight for overall victory.

LMP2

Private prototypes

The same Oreca chassis and Gibson V8 engine for everyone. Teams mix professional and amateur drivers.

LMGT3

Production-based cars

GT cars derived from road models (Ferrari 296, Porsche 911, Corvette…). Since 2024 they replace the GTE cars.

Hypercar

LMH or LMDh?

Two rule sets coexist in Hypercar. The aim: to attract more manufacturers at a reasonable cost.

LMH (Le Mans Hypercar)LMDh (Le Mans Daytona h)
ChassisDesigned freely by the manufacturerSupplied by one of 4 approved builders (Dallara, Ligier, Multimatic, Oreca)
HybridFree, often on the front axle (four-wheel drive)Common standard system, on the rear wheels
ExamplesToyota, Ferrari, Peugeot, Aston MartinBMW, Cadillac, Alpine, Genesis, Porsche
AdvantageMore technical freedomCheaper, and can also race in the USA (IMSA)
BoP

Balance of Performance (BoP)

So that very different cars can race together, the organisers adjust their minimum weight and maximum power before each race. A car that is too fast gets ballast, a car that is behind gets a little more power. As a result, lap time gaps are often just tenths of a second.

Drivers

Three drivers per car

👥

3 drivers

Each car is shared by three drivers who take turns over 24 hours.

⏱️

Max 4 h in 6 h

A driver cannot drive more than 4 hours in any 6-hour period.

🧮

Max 14 h in total

No driver may exceed 14 hours of driving over the whole race.

⛽

Regular stops

Roughly every 45 minutes to an hour, the car stops to refuel energy, change tyres or change driver.

🏁

The flags

Tap a card to see what it means.

🚨

Neutralising the race

🐢

Slow Zone

A Le Mans speciality: on one part of the track only, cars must drive at 80 km/h during an intervention.

🟡

Full Course Yellow

The whole track is limited to 80 km/h, with no overtaking.

🚗

Safety car

The circuit is so long that three safety cars go out at the same time, each gathering a group of cars behind it.

💡

Position lights

On the sides of the hypercars, lights show the position in class: 1 light for the leader, 2 for second, 3 for third. Handy for spectators at night!
